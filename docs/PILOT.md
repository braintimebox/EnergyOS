# EnergyOS — 14-Day Pilot Protocol

## Objective

Test whether a minimal, context-aware daily recommendation is usable and helpful in real life. The pilot evaluates feasibility and early signals, not clinical effectiveness.

## Design

### Days 1–3: baseline
Record the minimum inputs without deliberately changing routines:
- Sleep duration.
- Morning perceived energy (1–5).
- Work strain (1–5).
- Caffeine amount (mg).
- Optional: whether the day involved work.

Do not introduce a new set of goals during baseline.

### Days 4–7: one daily mission
Offer one small action based on transparent rules. User feedback after the action:
- Completed.
- Not feasible.
- Skipped.
- Optional helpfulness rating (1–5).

### Days 8–14: cautious adaptation
Use recorded feedback to choose among a small set of pre-reviewed recommendations. Change one recommendation policy at a time where possible.

## Primary measures

1. Check-in completion rate.
2. Median time needed for check-in.
3. Mission feasibility rate.
4. Mission completion rate.
5. Change in self-rated morning energy versus baseline.
6. User-reported burden caused by the app.

## Initial product thresholds

These are hypotheses, not externally validated cutoffs:
- Check-in time: <= 2 minutes per day; target <= 30 seconds for the quick check-in.
- Planned check-ins completed: >= 80%.
- Recommendations rated feasible: >= 70%.
- App-caused burden: no meaningful increase.
- Any change in morning energy is reported descriptively, not as proof that the app caused it.

## Daily protocol

### Morning (15–30 seconds)
1. Record sleep duration.
2. Rate perceived energy from 1–5.
3. Review one recommendation.

### During day
Log caffeine using quick-add controls if practical. Avoid interrupting hazardous or demanding work for data entry.

### After work (15–30 seconds)
1. Rate work strain from 1–5.
2. Mark mission completed / not feasible / skipped.
3. Optionally rate helpfulness.

If a second check-in feels burdensome, skip it; burden is itself a pilot finding.

## Weekly review

Ask:
- Which recommendations were realistic?
- Which were repeatedly impossible?
- Did the app create guilt or extra work?
- Were inputs accurate enough to guide decisions?
- Did the recommendations appear useful, neutral, or harmful?

## Stop / pause criteria

Pause the pilot if:
- Tracking causes noticeable stress or compulsive checking.
- Recommendations encourage unsafe work, driving, or sleep restriction.
- The user feels pressured to ignore illness, pain, or exhaustion to preserve XP.
- Data is unexpectedly shared or permission behaviour is unclear.

## Interpretation limits

A single-person 14-day pilot is exploratory. Sleep, workload, illness, weather, and other factors vary. Report observations and uncertainty; do not claim causal effectiveness.
